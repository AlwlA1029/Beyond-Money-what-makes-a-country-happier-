05 · KEY FINDINGS

What the data tells us

The data reveals three important patterns in the relationship between income and life evaluation.

01

Income matters, but it does not explain everything.

0.745

Correlation with life evaluation

The correlation between income-related contribution and life-evaluation scores is 0.745, indicating a positive relationship. However, this does not prove that higher income directly causes greater happiness.

02

Social support has a stronger correlation.

0.805

Correlation with life evaluation

The correlation between social-support contribution and life-evaluation scores is 0.805, higher than the 0.745 correlation for income-related contribution. This suggests that social support is also an important factor associated with life evaluation, although the comparison does not establish a greater causal effect.

03

Similar income contributions, different happiness scores.

3.975

Difference in life-evaluation scores

Costa Rica has an income-related contribution of 1.697 and a life-evaluation score of 7.439, while Botswana has corresponding values of 1.582 and 3.464. Their income-related contributions are relatively close, but their life-evaluation scores differ substantially. This highlights the limits of using income-related contribution alone to explain differences in happiness.

Note: Correlations describe relationships in the 2025 dataset; they do not establish cause and effect.

06 · CONCLUSION

Happiness is more than money.

Income-related contributions are associated with life evaluation, but they do not tell the whole story. Other factors offer additional perspectives on differences between countries.
